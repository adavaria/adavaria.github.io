Ali Davari, Ph.D.

Staff Data Scientist · University of Chicago

Collaborator, Chan Zuckerberg Biohub Chicago · Chicago, IL

I apply machine learning and data science to biology and materials science, building AI workflows that combine deep learning (generative diffusion, graph neural networks, protein language models) with physics-based simulation (DFT, MD). Much of my work is the data and evaluation behind the models: curating datasets, writing benchmarks, and testing how well models hold up on new data.

As a collaborator at CZ Biohub Chicago, I curated training data for and benchmarked DecoderTCR, a protein language model for TCR-pMHC recognition (ICML 2026). I also built a toolkit that benchmarks structure prediction models (AlphaFold2/3, Boltz, Protenix) on immune complexes, and I contribute to generative models for drug discovery. For my Ph.D. at the University of Illinois Chicago, I used 3D deep learning to predict the formation energy and synthesizability of crystalline materials.

Experience

University of Chicago

Collaborator, Chan Zuckerberg Biohub Chicago · Chicago, IL

  • Staff Data ScientistOct 2025 – Present
  • Research ScientistJul 2024 – Oct 2025
  • TCRkit & TCR-pMHC sequence catalog. Built TCRkit for full-length TCR and peptide-MHC sequence reconstruction, metrics, and QC. Used it to curate and integrate bound and unbound TCR, peptide, and MHC sequences into a full catalog of TCR-pMHC complexes for structure distillation and protein language model training.
  • DecoderTCR (ICML 2026). In collaboration with CZ Biohub Chicago, curated training data for successive versions of a masked protein language model for TCR-pMHC recognition and benchmarked each version (0.96 AUROC zero-shot pMHC binding; 0.76 AUROC epitope-specific TCR).
  • Protein structure prediction benchmarking toolkit (NeurIPS 2026 Workshop). Built a toolkit that benchmarks deep learning models on TCR-pMHC complex prediction and used it to evaluate 87 complexes released after the AlphaFold3 training cutoff.
    • Designed PDBFrame, a pandas-based Python object for efficient protein structure management and manipulation.
    • Automated chain type detection, gene/allele identification, and residue matching with truncation and missing-residue handling.
    • Benchmarked AlphaFold2/3, Boltz-1/2, TCRmodel2, Protenix, and ESMFold2 on immunology-specific metrics: RMSD, DockQ, LDDT, and docking angles.
    • Built a specialized database for MSA generation tailored to TCR-pMHC complexes.
  • Generative AI for drug discovery (HyDiF, NeurIPS 2025 Workshop). Contributed to a diffusion-guided hypernetwork that models small molecules as continuous neural fields, for conformer generation, inpainting for lead optimization, and property prediction.
  • Mentorship. Managed and mentored interns on interdisciplinary computational biology projects at CZ Biohub.

University of Illinois Chicago

Computational Materials Research Lab (CMRL), PI: Prof. Sara Kadkhodaei · Chicago, IL

  • Research / Teaching AssistantAug 2017 – Dec 2023
  • ML models for materials prediction. Improved the accuracy of 3D image-based deep learning models more than 7-fold (MAE 46 meV/atom). The result is the most accurate image-based model for the formation energy of crystalline materials.
  • Python toolkit for materials analysis. Developed and released a toolkit that uses 3D CNNs to analyze sparse point clouds and voxel images of crystals. Introduced a new model for the synthesizability of theoretical materials and applied it to battery electrode and thermoelectric materials (GitHub).
  • ML for superhard materials. Led the machine learning side of interdisciplinary collaborations predicting the properties and synthesis likelihood of next-generation superhard functional materials.
  • Ab initio simulations (VASP). Studied how anharmonic phonons affect diffusion in bcc metals, using temperature-dependent DFT and transition state theory. The simulated diffusion matched experimental data.
  • Climate statistics. Ran time-series and generalized extreme value (GEV) analyses of high-temperature extremes. Found that extreme-heat events double in frequency every 20 years and projected that they will occur yearly by 2100.
  • High-performance computing. Assembled a Condor HPC cluster from a dozen decommissioned nodes, which saved the lab a $20k HPC purchase.
  • Mentoring and teaching. Mentored new group members and supervised an undergraduate fluid mechanics laboratory.
